INTERLOCK: THE ER DIAGRAM IN SIMPLE WORDS
(goes with Interlock_ER_Diagram.png)

======================================================================
1. WHAT AN ER DIAGRAM IS
======================================================================
An ER (Entity-Relationship) diagram is a picture of what the database stores
and how those things are connected, drawn BEFORE making any tables.

  ENTITY        a "thing" we store facts about (a team, a part, a commit)
  ATTRIBUTE     a fact about that thing (a team's name, a part's number)
  RELATIONSHIP  how two things are connected (a team OWNS a part)

We use Chen notation, the standard textbook style.

======================================================================
2. THE SYMBOLS (only these are used)
======================================================================
  Rectangle           Entity, e.g. TEAM
  Double rectangle    Weak entity: cannot be identified on its own,
                      only through its owner (e.g. INTERFACE)
  Diamond             Relationship, e.g. OWNS
  Double diamond      Identifying relationship: the one that links a weak
                      entity to the owner it depends on
  Ellipse (oval)      Attribute
  Underlined name     Key attribute: unique, picks out exactly one row
  Dashed underline    Partial key: unique only inside its owner
  Double line         Total participation: EVERY row of that entity must
                      take part (every part MUST have an owning team)
  Single line         Partial participation: taking part is optional
  1 / N / M / P       Cardinality: how many on each side
                      1:N = one-to-many, M:N = many-to-many,
                      M:N:P = three-way (ternary)

How to read a line: "TEAM --1-- OWNS ==N== PART" means
one team owns many parts, and every part must be owned (double line).

======================================================================
3. THE ENTITIES (16)
======================================================================
TEAM          A group of engineers (chassis, drivetrain, standards,
              controls). Key: team_id.
PART          Something with a part number, e.g. BOLT-M6X20.
              Key: part_id. Owned by one team.
REVISION      One frozen version of a part. A change never edits a
              revision, it makes a new one. Key: revision_id.
SHAPE         One distinct piece of geometry, identified by its
              geometric "fingerprint". Many revisions can share it.
SHAPE_ALIAS   Another fingerprint that turned out to be the same shape
              (e.g. the same part exported by a different CAD tool).
FEATURE       A hole, flat face, boss or cone found on a shape.
COMMIT_LOG    One attempted change, with its verdict (landed/rejected)
              and the reason. Never deleted.
VALIDATION    One check that ran on a commit, and whether it passed.
CONTRACT      What a revision promises other teams: size envelope,
              maximum mass, hash of the promise.
INTERFACE     (weak) A mounting point a revision OFFERS, e.g. a bolt
              hole pattern. Identified by its revision + its name.
SOCKET        (weak) A mounting point an assembly REQUIRES something
              to fill. Identified by its revision + its name.
DIMENSION     (weak) A toleranced measurement on a revision
              (nominal +/- tolerance). Identified by revision + name.
CHAIN         A tolerance stack-up: several dimensions added together
              to check that a gap stays within limits.
REF           A named pointer ("main") to the current top assembly.
INTERFERENCE  A recorded clash between two placed parts (advice only).
OUTBOX        A queue of events waiting to be copied into the NoSQL
              document store. Connected to nothing on purpose: it is a
              delivery queue, not part of the data model.

======================================================================
4. THE RELATIONSHIPS (20)
======================================================================
 Relationship    Between                       Card.  Meaning
 --------------  ----------------------------  -----  --------------------------------
 OWNS            TEAM - PART                   1:N    a team owns many parts; every
                                                      part has an owner (total)
 HAS_REVISION    PART - REVISION               1:N    a part has many versions; every
                                                      revision belongs to a part
 INTRODUCES      COMMIT_LOG - REVISION         1:N    each revision was created by
                                                      exactly one commit
 HAS_SHAPE       SHAPE - REVISION              1:N    many revisions can share one
                                                      shape; pure assemblies have none
 CONTAINS        REVISION - REVISION           M:N    an assembly contains parts,
                                                      which may be assemblies too
                                                      (RECURSIVE: roles parent/child)
 HAS_CONTRACT    REVISION - CONTRACT           1:1    a revision has at most one
                                                      contract
 OFFERS          REVISION - INTERFACE          1:N    identifying (weak entity)
 REQUIRES        REVISION - SOCKET             1:N    identifying (weak entity)
 SPECIFIES       REVISION - DIMENSION          1:N    identifying (weak entity)
 INCLUDES        CHAIN - DIMENSION             M:N    a chain adds up many dimensions;
                                                      a dimension can be in many chains
 OWNS_CHAIN      TEAM - CHAIN                  1:N    optional owner of a chain
 SUBMITS         TEAM - COMMIT_LOG             1:N    a team makes many commits
 RECORDS         COMMIT_LOG - VALIDATION       1:N    one commit, many checks
 CLASSIFIES      COMMIT_LOG - PART             M:N    how disruptive a commit was for
                                                      each part it touched
 NOTIFIES        COMMIT_LOG - TEAM - PART      M:N:P  three-way: "this commit changed
                                                      this part, tell this team"
 SUBSCRIBES_TO   TEAM - PART                   M:N    a team says "I depend on this
                                                      part, warn me if it changes"
 ALIAS_OF        SHAPE - SHAPE_ALIAS           1:N
 HAS_FEATURE     SHAPE - FEATURE               1:N
 POINTS_TO       REVISION - REF                1:N    "main" points at one revision
 CHECKED_IN      REVISION - INTERFERENCE       1:N    clashes found in an assembly

Relationships can have their own attributes, drawn as ovals on the diamond:
  CONTAINS       quantity, instance_name, transform (position + rotation)
  INCLUDES       seq (order in the chain), direction (+ or -)
  NOTIFIES       reason, acknowledged
  CLASSIFIES     classification (no_op / internal / breaking)
  SUBSCRIBES_TO  interface_name

======================================================================
5. THE KEY IDEAS TO SAY IN THE VIVA
======================================================================
(a) Four separate things: PART, REVISION, SHAPE, and the CONTAINS link.
    Each one answers a different question:
      PART      WHO is it?           (part number, owner)
      REVISION  WHICH version?
      SHAPE     WHAT geometry?
      CONTAINS  WHERE, and HOW MANY? (position + quantity)
    So 27 identical bolts = 1 part, 1 revision, 1 shape, 27 placements.
    PLATE-A and PLATE-B have the same shape but are still two parts.

(b) Why quantity and position sit on CONTAINS, not on PART:
    the same bolt is used in many places, each with its own position.
    The fact belongs to the CONNECTION, not to the bolt.

(c) Recursive relationship: CONTAINS joins REVISION to REVISION.
    The roles "parent" and "child" say which end is which. This is how
    an assembly holds sub-assemblies to any depth.

(d) Weak entities: an interface called "foot" only makes sense on a
    particular revision. Its name is a partial key (dashed underline),
    unique only within that revision. Hence the double rectangle and the
    double diamond.

(e) Ternary relationship: NOTIFIES needs all three of commit, team and
    part at once. Splitting it into two-way links would lose which team
    was told about which part for which commit.

(f) Total vs partial: double line = must take part. Every revision MUST
    belong to a part and come from a commit. But a commit doesn't have to
    name a team, and a revision doesn't need a shape (an assembly has no
    geometry of its own), so those lines are single.

======================================================================
6. FROM THE DIAGRAM TO TABLES (relational schema)
======================================================================
Standard mapping rules, all applied:
  1. Each entity becomes a table; its key becomes the PRIMARY KEY.
  2. Weak entity: a table holding the owner's key as a FOREIGN KEY, with
     (owner key + partial key) UNIQUE. INTERFACE gets
     UNIQUE(revision_id, name).
  3. 1:N: put the "1" side's key as a foreign key on the "N" side.
     e.g. OWNS becomes part.team_id.
  4. 1:1: foreign key on the total side, marked UNIQUE.
     e.g. HAS_CONTRACT becomes contract.revision_id UNIQUE.
  5. M:N: becomes its own table holding both keys.
     CONTAINS -> occurrence(parent_rev, child_rev, quantity, ...)
     INCLUDES -> chain_member(chain_id, seq, dimension_id, direction)
     CLASSIFIES -> change_classification(commit_id, part_id, ...)
     SUBSCRIBES_TO -> subscription(team_id, part_id, interface_name)
  6. Ternary: its own table with three foreign keys.
     NOTIFIES -> notification(commit_id, team_id, part_id, ...)

Result: 16 entity tables + 5 relationship tables = 21 tables, matching
schema.sql exactly.

One detail worth knowing: occurrence has its own id (occurrence_id)
rather than using (parent_rev, child_rev) as its key, because the SAME
child can sit in the SAME parent twice at different positions
(two identical brackets, left and right).

Some rules can't be drawn on an ER diagram, so the database enforces them
with triggers:
  - an assembly may never contain itself, at any depth
  - revisions and placements can't be edited, only replaced
  - the commit log can't be deleted from
